## Design decisions

<div class="card">

**The core approach:** I prototyped two very different versions of the main interaction before picking one.

- **Option A** felt more intuitive to new players, but had a low skill ceiling.
- **Option B** took longer to learn, but rewarded precision in a way that felt more satisfying once it clicked.

**Pacing:** I iterated on difficulty escalation several times based on playtester reactions.

- First instinct: ramp difficulty quickly.
- What playtesters showed me: most people were still learning the core interaction well past when I expected mastery.
- Final approach: a noticeably slower ramp than my first instinct.

</div>

## Technique highlights

<details class="accordion" open>
<summary>Landmarks</summary>
<div class="card">

**My approach:** I used key landmarks to support navigation and give players clear points of orientation.

**Level examples:**

- **Main goal:** the long-term objective and primary world anchor.
- **Windmill:** one of the required locations and a regional identifier.
- **Clock tower:** a central hub landmark used for orientation.

</div>
</details>

<details class="accordion">
<summary>Looping paths</summary>
<div class="card">

**My approach:** I used looping paths to reduce backtracking, so exploration stays smooth and rewarding.

**How I applied it:** I added loops in several areas so players can rejoin the main path after optional content.

</div>
</details>

## What I learned

<div class="card">

**Playtesting changes assumptions:** several decisions that felt obvious to me were confusing for first-time players.

**Scope matters:** keeping the prototype tightly focused on one mechanic made it much easier to finish and learn from.

- Capture screenshots and notes **during** development
- Test with **fresh players** early
- Keep each prototype focused on **one question**

</div>
